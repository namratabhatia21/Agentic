# Lesson design

## Lesson template (Gagné's nine events)

Every lesson file follows this shape. Gagné's events are in brackets. Headings can be
renamed for the audience, but keep the order.

```markdown
# <Lesson title>

## Why this matters
<1-3 sentences: a relatable problem, question or surprising fact.>  [1 Gain attention]

**By the end of this lesson you can** <objective in plain words>.  [2 State objectives]

## Start from what you know
<Link to the previous lesson or everyday experience.>  [3 Recall prior learning]

## <Core content, in 2-4 headed chunks>
<Short paragraphs, a table or diagram, one idea per chunk.>  [4 Present content]

## Worked example
<A realistic example, worked step by step, with reasoning visible.>  [5 Guide learning]

## Try it
<A short task or question at the objective's Bloom level.>  [6 Elicit performance]
<The answer, with why it's right and the common mistake.>  [7 Give feedback]

## Key takeaways
<3-5 bullets learners could put on a sticky note.>  [9 Retention and transfer]

*Source: S1, <section/page>.*
```

Event 8, **assess performance**, happens in the module quiz and the final assessment, not
inside the lesson.

For **Apply-level and higher** objectives, use **Merrill's First Principles**. Centre the
lesson on a real task and activate prior knowledge. Demonstrate with a worked example,
have learners apply it with fading help, and close with how they'll use it at work.

## Chunking and length

- 5-15 minutes per lesson (about 600-1,500 words of text, or a 3-8 minute video plus
  text). Split anything longer.
- One idea per chunk. If a heading needs "and", consider two chunks.
- Introduce at most 3-5 new terms per lesson. Define them where they first appear, and
  add them to the glossary.
- Move from concrete to abstract: example first, then the rule.

## Mayer's multimedia principles

| Principle | Do this |
|---|---|
| Coherence | Cut interesting-but-irrelevant material: decorative images, tangents, background music |
| Signaling | Use headings, bold key terms and "first / then / finally" to show structure |
| Redundancy | Don't put the full narration on screen as text; use short labels |
| Spatial contiguity | Put labels on the diagram, not in a legend |
| Temporal contiguity | Narrate at the moment the visual appears |
| Segmenting | Split into learner-paced parts with a "next" step |
| Pre-training | Teach key terms before the process that uses them |
| Modality | For animations, explain with narration rather than on-screen text |
| Personalization | Use a conversational tone ("you", "we"), not formal passive voice |

## Video script format

Two columns, with roughly 130-150 spoken words per minute:

```markdown
| Visual | Narration |
|---|---|
| Title card: "Why context changes everything" | Two people can type almost the same request... |
| Side-by-side: two prompts and their answers | Look at what changed when we added one sentence... |
```

## Activities by Bloom level

| Level | Activity ideas |
|---|---|
| Remember | Flashcards, matching, label-the-diagram, quick recall quiz |
| Understand | Explain-it-back, sort examples vs. non-examples, summarize in 2 sentences |
| Apply | Worked problem then practice set, simulation, do-it-on-your-own-data task |
| Analyze | Case study, find-the-error, compare two cases, root-cause exercise |
| Evaluate | Critique with a checklist, rank options and justify, peer review with a rubric |
| Create | Mini-project, design brief, plan or template for own context |

Each activity needs: a title, the objective(s) it serves, minutes, clear instructions,
and what the learner produces or how they check themselves.

## Accessibility and inclusion (UDL, WCAG basics)

- Use a real heading structure (H1 per lesson, then H2/H3), and write descriptive link
  text ("the GDPR summary", not "click here").
- Give images alt text that says what the image *shows* and why it matters. Mark
  decorative images as decorative.
- Give video captions and a transcript, and audio a transcript.
- Don't rely on colour alone to carry meaning. Aim for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.
- Use plain language: short sentences, define jargon, and aim at the audience's reading
  level.
- Offer more than one way in (text + visual + example) and more than one way to show
  learning where possible.
- Use varied, realistic names, places and scenarios in examples. Avoid stereotypes.
